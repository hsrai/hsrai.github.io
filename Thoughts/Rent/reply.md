In the simplest possible words:

Your reply says:

> "Don't reject my case now. We need a full trial first."

The respondent says:

> "You don't need a trial. Even if everything written by the petitioner is accepted as true, the law itself says this case cannot be filed under the Punjab Rent Act."

The judge's first question will therefore be very simple:

> "Can I decide this by only reading the plaint and the law?"

If the answer is "Yes", then there is no need for a trial.

Now comes the important part.

You told me that:

* the building was completed in 2021; and
* this date is not disputed.

If that is correct, then there is no factual dispute about the date.

So your reply repeatedly says:

> "This requires evidence."

But the judge may think:

> "Evidence about what? Everyone agrees the building was completed in 2021."

This is the weak point in your reply.

Imagine a simple example.

Suppose a law says:

> "Children below 18 cannot vote."

A person files a case saying:

> "I am 16 years old. Let me vote."

The government replies:

> "Age is a mixed question of law and fact. We need a full trial."

The judge may answer:

> "No. You yourself say you are 16. I don't need witnesses. The law is clear."

Your case could face the same difficulty.

The judge may ask:

> "If the plaint itself shows the building was completed in 2021, and Section 3 exempts such buildings for 15 years, why should I conduct a trial?"

That is the question your reply does not answer.

Your reply mainly argues:

* don't believe the respondent,
* don't decide facts now,
* evidence is needed.

But if there is no dispute about the completion date, these arguments become much less convincing.

How good is the reply?

* Structure: 9/10
* Language: 8/10
* Legal strategy: about 5/10 (based on the facts you've told me)

The weakness is not the drafting. The weakness is that the reply avoids answering the main issue:

> "If the building was completed in 2021, why does the Punjab Rent Act still apply?"

Unless there is a legal reason why Section 3 does not apply despite the 2021 completion, the court may not be persuaded merely by saying that evidence is required.

One more thing concerns me. Earlier, you mentioned this was an "eviction petition." Under the Punjab Rent Act, such proceedings are ordinarily before the Rent Controller, not by way of a civil suit. You have titled the pleading as a reply to an application under Order VII Rule 11 CPC and referred to a "plaint." Whether Order VII Rule 11 CPC is even the correct procedural provision depends on the nature of the proceedings. That procedural context could itself become important.
